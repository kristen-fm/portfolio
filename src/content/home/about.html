My professional journey began in South Korea, where I spent two years teaching English and discovered my passion for education. That experience inspired me to pursue a master's degree in English with a focus on teaching English as a second or foreign language. I subsequently taught university-level writing at Colorado State University, working with both domestic and international students.

In 2016, I transitioned into software development, eventually moving into engineering leadership and Developer Experience roles. At Pluralsight, I designed technical learning experiences for the Software Engineering organization, helping developers build their skills and grow professionally. I later embedded with the Developer Success Lab, where I participated in empirical research on developer experiences and helped translate research findings into practical educational materials, workshops, and interventions intended to support positive behavioral change in software teams.

Today, I bring together my backgrounds in education, software engineering, and research as a Technical Curriculum Developer at Datadog, where I design hands-on learning experiences for software practitioners. My interests include adult learning theory, learner motivation and engagement, self-directed learning, and the relationship between research and educational practice. I'm especially interested in how we can translate empirical research into better learning experiences outside traditional educational institutions.

I live in the Colorado mountains, where I spend a great deal of my free time running, hiking, and exploring the outdoors. I'm an ultramarathon runner and certified ultrarunning coach, and I love helping other runners understand the why behind their training. I'm also an enthusiastic reader with an enduring curiosity about philosophy, history, and the social sciences. And I'm a lifelong learner in the most literal sense: Whether I'm studying a new subject or experimenting with a new skill, learning is one of the great pleasures of my life.
