Your Software Engineers Do Need Communication Skills

An engineering manager argued his developers should just build things and leave the communicating to others. This reflection pushes back: SEs communicate constantly — code reviews, RFCs, explaining delays — and communication failures carry real costs. The way forward is to appeal to engineers' analytic mindsets by teaching them to analyze “rhetorical situations” (author, audience, text, purpose, context).

Technical Upskilling: Are We Walking in Circles without a Rhetorical Compass?

Software engineers sign up for upskilling enthusiastically — then don't show up, don't do the work, or don't finish. Drawing on Barbara Schneider's work on professional development and Lloyd Bitzer's rhetorical situation, this reflection argues that engagement depends on making the “why” of upskilling clear and issuing a genuine invitation into the learning, not an order from above.

Learn How to Code: Insights from the L2 Motivational Self System

Learning to code is hard, but it's achievable — and what often separates those who persist from those who give up is motivation. This reflection borrows Zoltán Dörnyei's L2 Motivational Self System from second language acquisition research and asks how the “ideal” and “ought-to” selves might explain who sticks with learning to program.
